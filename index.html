Things worth asking
  • Ask your case manager or foster parent how much allowance or personal-needs money you get, and when. Write it down.
  • Pay from your own job is yours. If anyone asks you to hand it over, ask your case manager to explain the rule.
  • If Social Security benefits are paid because a parent died or because of a disability, ask who the "representative payee" is and whether any of that money is being saved for you.
  • Foster Success pays Indiana youth with foster care experience for passing its free online money modules (80% or higher). Ask your Independent Living Specialist how to sign up.

Your first bank account

Most banks won't open an account for someone under 18 alone. Here's how it usually works, and what to check for.

  1. Find your adult co-owner. A foster parent, a relative, or another trusted adult can be the joint owner until you're 18. Ask your case manager who is allowed in your placement.
  2. Pick a credit union or bank with a teen or student account. Look for no monthly fee, no minimum balance, a free debit card, an app, and an ATM near you.
  3. Bring: your ID (or school ID plus the adult's ID), your Social Security number, and proof of address. DCS can give you a letter if you need one.
  4. Ask five questions: Is there a monthly fee? A minimum balance? Overdraft fees? Can I turn overdraft off? What changes when I turn 18?
  5. At 18, open your own account or have the co-owner removed, then move your direct deposit.

    Indiana programs

    Money and support you may already qualify for.

    Each card links to the official page. Dollar amounts are the maximums the program sets, not promises, and rules change. When in doubt, ask the contacts at the bottom.

    DCS · from age 16

    Older Youth Services

    Independent living skill-building in money, housing, school, and work for youth in foster care from 16. Transition-to-adulthood services start at 17½ if you're expected to age out.

    DCS Older Youth Initiatives ↗
    DCS · ages 18 to 21

    Collaborative Care

    Indiana's extended foster care. Rent and utilities, a clothing allowance, health coverage, continued services, and a case manager while you're in school, in a work program, or working 80+ hours a month. You can re-enter before 20.

    Collaborative Care fact sheet (PDF) ↗
    DCS · to age 23

    Voluntary Services

    Support up to the day before your 23rd birthday if you aged out at 18 after six months or more in care, finished Collaborative Care at 21, or were adopted or placed in guardianship at 16 or older.

    Voluntary Services at DCS ↗
    Foster Success · under 26

    Education and Training Voucher (ETV)

    Up to $5,000 per school year for college, trade school, or training if you left care at 18 or later, or were adopted or in guardianship after 16. Start before 21 to stay eligible until 26. Keep a 2.0 GPA.

    Indiana ETV ↗
    Indiana CHE · high school

    21st Century Scholars

    Up to four years of tuition at eligible Indiana colleges. Youth in foster care can enroll after 8th grade and skip the income test. File the FAFSA by April 15 of senior year and every year after.

    21st Century Scholars ↗
    Foster Success · ages 18 to 25

    Opportunity Passport

    Free money classes, $100 to start saving, $80 per survey twice a year, and a $1-for-$1 match up to $3,500 lifetime for rent and deposit, a car, school costs, credit building, and more.

    Opportunity Passport ↗
    Foster Success

    Online modules and Credit Build

    Get paid for passing free online money courses with 80% or higher, then a 12-month Credit Build program to start or repair your credit while you save.

    Online financial modules ↗
    DCS · from age 14

    Free yearly credit report

    While you're in care, DCS pulls your credit report every year from 14 and helps fix mistakes. At 18 to 21 you get all three bureaus through your Older Youth Services provider.

    DCS credit reporting ↗
    Indiana Medicaid · to age 26

    Health coverage to 26

    Former foster youth can keep Medicaid until 26. Keep your card, and update your address whenever you move so renewals reach you.

    Car insurance help

    The Insuring Foster Youth Trust Fund helps youth in care with car insurance so a license can lead to a job. Ask your Older Youth Case Manager or Independent Living Specialist.

    About the project

    A small project with a meaningful purpose.

    The Next Chapter Project is an independent student-led educational initiative in its early pilot stage. It aims to make practical money knowledge easier to reach, with particular attention to young people in Indiana foster care and those adopted from care.

    We're developing these resources and hope to improve them with feedback from young adults and community organizations. No partnerships or program outcomes are claimed at this stage.

    Not in Indiana?

    Every state runs Chafee, ETV, and extended foster care differently. The tools above still work; the program cards are Indiana-specific. Start with your state's child welfare agency or the resources below.

    Meet the founder

    Why I started The Next Chapter Project

    I'm Harris Kirmani, from Houston, Texas, and a student at Indiana University Bloomington. I've always cared about helping others through volunteering, community service, and mentoring younger students.

    As someone who was adopted, I appreciate the importance of support, stability, and opportunity. My experience is different from that of young people transitioning out of foster care, but it helped shape my motivation to make a difference.

    In college, I wanted to use my education and interest in business to build something that could reach more people. I started The Next Chapter Project to make financial literacy and practical life skills more accessible, especially to young people preparing for independence.

    I'm learning from foster care professionals, educators, and young people as the project grows. Every young person deserves the opportunity to write their next chapter.
